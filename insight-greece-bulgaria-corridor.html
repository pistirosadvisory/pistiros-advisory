The Greece–Bulgaria corridor: Alexandroupolis, gas and the rail link to the Black Sea

Greece and Bulgaria are building a north–south corridor that connects Aegean ports with the Black Sea and the Danube without passing through the Bosporus. It already carries gas and military logistics; rail freight is slower to arrive. For buyers, it offers an alternative route into and out of Bulgaria, and a way to diversify away from the Turkish land border.

October 2026 · This article is in English.

The three strands

1. Gas: the Vertical Gas Corridor

Since Russia cut supplies in 2022, Bulgaria has relied heavily on gas arriving from Greece, including Azerbaijani pipeline gas and LNG. In June 2026 the Bulgarian and Greek prime ministers said Bulgaria was working to raise the capacity of its pipeline link with Greece by 2 billion cubic metres to 5 billion, with works in southern Bulgaria complete and northern works under way. US LNG landed in Greece is to flow on through Bulgaria and Romania to Ukraine, with reverse flows also possible (Pipeline & Gas Journal, citing Reuters).

For energy-intensive buyers in Bulgaria, this is the main source of supply security, and its capacity limits how much industrial gas demand can grow.

2. Military logistics: Alexandroupolis

The port of Alexandroupolis, near the Turkish border, became a NATO logistics gateway after US forces began landing equipment there to move it by rail and road to eastern Europe without using the Bosporus. A 2021 deployment moved about 1,000 pieces of US equipment through the port in under two weeks, and the port made its first ship-to-train transfer to Constanța in Romania in 2020 (ICWA).

That role turns the road and rail links from Alexandroupolis into Bulgaria into strategic infrastructure, with security and reliability expectations to match.

3. Rail freight: the "Sea2Sea" link

Greece and Bulgaria plan a rail freight corridor from Thessaloniki, Kavala and Alexandroupolis to Burgas, Varna and the Danube port of Ruse. Greece budgeted about €4 billion for rail investment on its side, and its rail infrastructure company estimated that about €650 million of container cargo a year could shift from sea routes through the Bosporus to rail, with Greek works expected around 2027–2028 (RailFreight). In June 2026 the prime ministers reaffirmed support for the rail corridor, and Bulgaria expressed interest in a role at the port of Kavala (Pipeline & Gas Journal).

Bulgarian rail lags behind. Slow sections, limited electrification and old rolling stock on the Bulgarian side mean the corridor's full capacity will depend on investment that has not yet been delivered.

Why it matters for buyers

  • An alternative to Kapitan Andreevo. Goods moving between Bulgaria and the Mediterranean, or between Turkey's western region and the EU, can route through Greek ports instead of the congested land border.
  • Schengen with Greece. Since January 2025 there are no routine border checks between Bulgaria and Greece, so the corridor is a single internal market leg.
  • Energy security. Industrial users depend on the gas corridor's capacity.
  • Defence and strategic supply. Military traffic on the same routes brings heightened security, but also priority use at times.

Risks

  • Delivery risk. Rail timelines have slipped repeatedly on both sides.
  • Capacity conflicts. Military, energy and commercial traffic compete for the same ports and lines.
  • Port ownership. Concessions at Greek ports have changed hands; check who operates a terminal before relying on it.
  • Security. Critical infrastructure in the region faces sabotage risk, as shown by attacks on communications infrastructure in Bulgaria in 2026.

What this means for buyers

  • Would a Greek port plus road or rail into Bulgaria be faster or more reliable than the Turkish land border for your goods?
  • Does your energy supply depend on the gas corridor, and what happens if flows are interrupted?
  • Who operates the terminal and rail services you would use, and how reliable are they today?
  • Could military priority traffic affect your logistics at short notice?
