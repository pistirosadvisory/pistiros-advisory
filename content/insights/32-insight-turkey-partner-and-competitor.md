# Turkey as partner and competitor

Summary: Turkey is Bulgaria's largest neighbour, a top trading partner, a growing investor and, increasingly, a competitor for European manufacturing and defence work. It is also the main land route between the EU and the Middle East, the Caucasus and Central Asia. Buyers who source from or through Bulgaria need a view on Turkey.
Published: October 2026
Date: 2026-10-25
Category: regional

## Partner

- **Trade.** Bulgaria–Turkey trade reached a record €9 billion in 2025, up 18% on 2024, according to Bulgaria's deputy economy minister ([BTA](https://www.bta.bg/en/news/economy/1110562-bulgaria-turkiye-trade-has-reached-record-setting-eur-9-bln)). Turkey is among the main markets for Bulgarian exports, including copper, soda ash and rice.
- **Investment.** Turkish direct investment in Bulgaria exceeded €1.5 billion by the end of 2025, in industry, construction, automotive, textiles, and food and beverages ([BTA](https://www.bta.bg/en/news/economy/1110562-bulgaria-turkiye-trade-has-reached-record-setting-eur-9-bln)).
- **Customs union.** Most Turkish industrial goods enter the EU duty-free, which makes Bulgarian–Turkish supply chains straightforward on tariffs, though not at the border, where queues at Kapitan Andreevo and Lesovo can run for days.
- **People.** Bulgaria has a large Turkish-speaking minority, and many Bulgarian businesses have Turkish partners, customers or owners.

## Competitor

**Manufacturing.** Turkey exported a record $396 billion of goods in 2025, about $117 billion of it to the EU ([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/trade/2026/01/turkeys-exports-hit-396bn-in-2025-says-erdogan/)). With a population over ten times Bulgaria's and a deep industrial base in textiles, automotive, white goods and steel, Turkey competes directly with Bulgaria for European nearshoring orders. Bulgaria's advantages are EU membership, the euro and EU product law; Turkey's are scale and price.

**Defence.** Turkey's defence exports reached about $10 billion in 2025, a record and roughly 50% up on 2024, led by rockets, missiles and smart munitions ($3.7 billion) and drones ($2.1 billion). In the first seven months of 2026 they rose a further 26% to $5.79 billion ([Al-Monitor](https://al-monitor.com/originals/2026/08/turkeys-defense-exports-reach-record-579b-first-7-months-2026)). Turkish firms are now winning in NATO Europe: Aselsan signed a $410 million electronic-warfare contract with Poland in December 2025, and STM agreed to export loitering munitions to an unnamed EU and NATO member ([Al-Monitor](https://al-monitor.com/originals/2026/08/turkeys-defense-exports-reach-record-579b-first-7-months-2026)).

Bulgaria's ammunition industry and Turkey's are competing for the same European demand. Bulgaria has EU membership and EU defence funding on its side; Turkey has scale, an integrated industry and fast delivery.

## Risks for buyers

- **Sanctions divergence.** Turkey has not imposed EU sanctions on Russia. Turkish companies can lawfully trade with Russia, and Western enforcement agencies have repeatedly found EU-origin goods passing through Turkey to Russia. A Bulgarian supplier with large Turkish customers needs end-user checks.
- **Diverted grain and goods.** Ukraine's intelligence service named Turkey among the destinations for grain taken from occupied Ukraine in 2025; similar claims have been made about other goods.
- **Currency and inflation.** High Turkish inflation and a weak lira make Turkish suppliers price-competitive but contract terms and payment risk harder to manage.
- **Origin.** Goods made partly outside Turkey may not qualify for customs-union treatment; check A.TR certificates and origin rules.
- **Defence export controls.** Turkish components in Bulgarian products, or Bulgarian components in Turkish systems, can complicate licensing and end-use assurances for European customers.

## Opportunities

- **Joint supply chains** where Bulgaria provides EU-compliant final assembly or certification for Turkish-made parts, and Turkish partners provide scale.
- **Logistics and border-region industrial zones**, which both governments have promoted.
- **Gateway to the east**: Turkish partners' networks in the Caucasus, Central Asia and the Middle East, used with proper checks.

## What this means for buyers

- If your Bulgarian supplier sells heavily to Turkey, do you know who the final users are?
- Do your Turkish-Bulgarian supply chains meet customs-union origin rules?
- Are you comparing Bulgarian and Turkish quotes on the full landed cost, including border delays and currency risk?
- For defence work, does Turkish content affect your customer's acceptance or your licences?
