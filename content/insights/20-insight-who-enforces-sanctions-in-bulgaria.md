# Who enforces sanctions in Bulgaria

Summary: EU sanctions apply directly in every member state, but enforcement is national. In Bulgaria the system is spread across several agencies, the criminal law has lagged behind EU requirements, and the rules for companies changed in 2025. This note sets out who does what and what is changing.
Published: October 2026
Date: 2026-10-25
Category: regulatory

## Who does what

Responsibility is split by type of measure. In outline:

- **Customs Agency.** Applies trade restrictions at the border: banned goods, controlled items, origin and transit checks. The Kapitan Andreevo crossing with Turkey is the main pressure point.
- **Ministry of Economy and its export-control commission.** Licenses dual-use and defence exports and decides on authorisations that sanctions regulations allow ([export control act](https://exportcontrol.mi.government.bg/docs/DefenceRelated_Products_and_DualUse_Items_and_Technologies_Export_Control_Act_20.02.2015.pdf)).
- **State Agency for National Security (DANS).** Investigates serious cases, including the 2023 Plovdiv dual-use scheme, and houses the financial intelligence unit that receives suspicious-transaction reports.
- **Bulgarian National Bank and the Financial Supervision Commission.** Supervise how banks, insurers and investment firms apply asset freezes and screening.
- **Prosecutor's Office and courts.** Bring and decide criminal cases.
- **Ministry of Foreign Affairs.** Coordinates Bulgaria's position on EU sanctions.

For a buyer, the practical point is that there is no single sanctions authority to ask, as there is with the UK's OFSI or the US OFAC. Guidance is thinner and practice varies.

## The criminal-law gap

EU Directive 2024/1226 requires member states to make serious sanctions breaches, including circumvention, a crime. The transposition deadline was 20 May 2025. Bulgaria missed it, and the European Commission opened an infringement procedure ([BTA](https://bta.bg/en/news/bulgaria/1193392-government-approves-bill-making-violations-of-eu-restrictive-measures-serious-cr)).

On 27 August 2026 the government approved a bill to close the gap. It would criminalise ([BTA](https://bta.bg/en/news/bulgaria/1193392-government-approves-bill-making-violations-of-eu-restrictive-measures-serious-cr)):

- breaching asset freezes and travel bans;
- concluding or continuing prohibited transactions, including public procurement and concession contracts;
- providing banned financial services;
- providing banned non-financial services such as legal, trust, accounting and auditing services;
- circumvention, as a separate offence.

The bill uses the directive's option of a €10,000 threshold: smaller violations would not be criminal. Penalties include prison, fines and loss of certain rights, though published reports did not give the amounts. It passed first reading in parliament on 24 September 2026, with opposition criticism ([BTA](https://www.bta.bg/en/news/bulgaria/1210620-criminal-code-revisions-criminalize-commercial-activities-that-could-be-linked-t)). Check whether it has been finally adopted.

## Corporate liability since October 2025

Separately, a 2025 reform introduced what lawyers describe as quasi-criminal liability for companies, in force from 31 October 2025 ([Wolf Theiss](https://www.wolftheiss.com/insights/bulgaria-to-introduce-quasi-criminal-corporate-liability-for-legal-entities)):

- It covers a broad list of offences, including corruption, money laundering, fraud, tax and customs offences.
- A company can be sanctioned for acts of its managers, employees or agents, even if no individual is convicted.
- Where the company benefited, fines can reach BGN 10 million (about €5.1 million), or the value of the benefit or 5% of prior-year turnover if higher.
- Courts can also ban a company from public procurement, concessions and EU funding for one to three years, and confiscate proceeds.
- There is no formal compliance defence, but effective compliance and cooperation can reduce the sanction.

Customs offences are already covered, so a company that evades sanctions through misdeclared exports may face this regime now, before the new sanctions crimes are in force.

## What this means in practice

- **Enforcement has been uneven.** The few public cases, such as the Plovdiv scheme in 2023, came from security-service investigations rather than routine supervision.
- **Exposure is rising.** A corporate-liability regime in force and a sanctions-crime bill in parliament mean Bulgarian companies face more risk than two years ago. Well-run suppliers will respond with better compliance; others may not.
- **Your home rules still apply.** UK and US companies remain subject to their own sanctions laws, which reach conduct abroad and through intermediaries.

## What this means for buyers

- Does your Bulgarian supplier know that sanctions breaches and circumvention are becoming crimes, with corporate liability?
- Who in the supplier is responsible for sanctions screening, and could they describe the process?
- Has the supplier been subject to a customs or security-service investigation?
- If something went wrong, which Bulgarian authority would you report to, and would your home regulator expect you to report too?
