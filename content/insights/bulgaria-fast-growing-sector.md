# Software and IT in Bulgaria: what the evidence shows, and what buyers should ask

Published: October 2026
Date: 2026-10-17
Category: sectors

Summary: Software and IT services is Bulgaria's best-documented growth sector, but its strengths and its risks both sit in a small number of foreign-owned employers and a tight talent pool.


## Why we chose this sector

We considered four candidates: software and IT, electronics and automotive components, pharma, and logistics and ports. Software and IT has the most consistent, regularly published data. The industry association BASSCOM publishes an annual survey, and the EU has named a Bulgarian site for one of its AI factories. We have since covered [automotive and electronics](insight-bulgaria-automotive-electronics.html) and logistics, in our [Silk Road note](insight-bulgaria-and-the-silk-road.html); pharma is left for a later note.

## Scale and growth

BASSCOM's 2025 Barometer, reported by the Bulgarian news agency BTA, says sector revenue grew 11.5% in 2024. It forecasts a further 9% for 2025, taking the industry to roughly BGN 11 billion. The same report counts about 6,100 companies and more than 60,000 employees. About 87% of revenue comes from exports.

On share of the economy, the figures differ. A Capital.bg report on the 2024 Barometer puts software revenue at 4.9% of GDP in 2023. BASSCOM's own site shows 4.5% for the same year. We could not find a confirmed 2025 figure. Revenue as a share of GDP is also not the same as value added, so treat these as approximate.

Other counts are larger because they define the sector more widely. The US government's country guide, published in April 2026, cites about 70,000 ICT jobs, or 2.3% of total employment, against an EU average of 3.5%, citing Eurostat. The 2.3% figure may be an older Eurostat estimate, so check it before relying on it.

## Who is there

DEV.BG's March 2025 ranking of IT employers lists DXC Technology (3,439 IT staff), SAP Labs Bulgaria (1,700), Hewlett Packard Enterprise (1,500), VMware Bulgaria (1,300) and DraftKings (1,000). Headcounts are for Bulgarian teams. An earlier DEV.BG analysis found that 64% of the top 100 IT employers are foreign-owned. Progress (Telerik) is also a large Sofia presence, with more than 600 staff, according to the same coverage.

Sofia dominates. One analysis found 94% of the top employers have offices there; Plovdiv (31%) and Varna (23%) follow.

On the research side, INSAIT, a computer science and AI institute, and Sofia Tech Park jointly won one of six new EU AI factories. The project is called BRAIN++.

## AI and compute

The EuroHPC programme selected Bulgaria, Austria, France, Germany, Poland and Slovenia for AI factories. BRAIN++ has a budget of EUR 90 million, with the Bulgarian government committing 50% as national co-financing. Its core is Discoverer++, a new supercomputer aimed at AI workloads, with over 7.5 PB of storage. Construction is due to start in 2026 with a three-year implementation period. These are plans, not operating capacity. Buyers should not assume the capacity exists yet.

## Why it grew

Sources point to a few factors:

- Cost and talent. The US country guide describes qualified IT specialists as relatively inexpensive. It puts a typical highly qualified specialist at around USD 20,000 a year, two to three times the average Bulgarian wage. Vendor blogs claim savings of 40-60% against Western Europe; those claims come from firms selling outsourcing, so we do not rely on them.
- Tax. Corporate income tax is a flat 10%, according to the same guide.
- EU membership. Bulgaria sits inside the EU single market and its data protection regime (GDPR), which matters to Western clients. This is our reading rather than a sourced finding.

## What the euro and Schengen change

Bulgaria adopted the euro on 1 January 2026, as the 21st euro-area member, after the European Commission and ECB found it met the criteria. For IT exporters billing in euros, this removes currency conversion on a large part of their sales. We did not find a study quantifying the effect.

Bulgaria applied Schengen rules at air and sea borders from 31 March 2024, and land border checks were lifted after a Council decision in December 2024. This matters little for software, which moves over networks. It matters more for hardware, logistics and staff travel.

## Constraints

- Talent supply. The US guide says IT demand may reach three times what institutions can supply. The BASSCOM-reported pay gap between Sofia and other regions is already narrowing to 5-7%.
- Wages. One vendor source cites annual wage growth of 8-12% in knowledge roles. Treat that as indicative only.
- Concentration. A few large foreign employers account for much of the headcount, and Sofia dominates. A decision at a parent company abroad can change local employment quickly.
- Infrastructure. The US guide notes that only 17% of rural areas have high-speed broadband, against 87.3% of urban homes.

## What this means for buyers and counterparties

Questions we would ask before contracting with a Bulgarian software or IT firm:

- Ownership. Who ultimately owns and controls the company, and has that changed recently? A Sofia office can be a branch of a group whose real decisions are taken elsewhere.
- Intellectual property. Do employment and contractor agreements assign IP to the company? Many developers work as self-employed contractors, so check the paper trail.
- Data location. Where are code, customer data and backups held, and which cloud providers and sub-processors have access?
- Subcontractor chains. How much of the work is passed to other firms or freelancers, and in which countries? Can the supplier name them?
- Sanctions exposure. Have the company, its owners, its clients and key staff been screened against EU, UK and US sanctions lists? Does the firm serve clients in sanctioned jurisdictions?

Why this matters beyond the sector: software is one of the few Bulgarian industries already growing at something like a "tiger" pace, and whether it can move from outsourcing to owning products is one of the questions in [Is Bulgaria a Balkan Tiger?](insight-is-bulgaria-a-balkan-tiger.html).
