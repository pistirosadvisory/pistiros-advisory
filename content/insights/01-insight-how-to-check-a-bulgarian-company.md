# How to check a Bulgarian company in 30 minutes

Summary: Bulgaria's company register is one of the most open in Europe: the record, the board and the filed accounts are free to read without an account. The obstacles are language, a few blind spots, and knowing which signals matter.
Published: October 2026
Date: 2026-10-25
Category: guides

## Why the register is worth your time

Since 1 January 2008, every Bulgarian company has sat in a single electronic database run by the Registry Agency, which replaced the separate registers previously kept by 28 provincial courts. Anyone can search it at portal.registryagency.bg in a Bulgarian or English interface. The English version translates menus and field labels only; names, addresses and documents stay in Bulgarian.

For a foreign buyer, that means a first-pass check costs nothing but time. The sequence below takes about 30 minutes for a straightforward supplier. It is a screen, not a full due-diligence review.

## Minutes 0–5: pin down the identity

Ask the supplier for its UIC (ЕИК), the 9-digit unified identification code. It is set when the company is first entered and does not change until the company is deleted. Companies that existed before 2008 kept their old BULSTAT code as their UIC. A branch of a Bulgarian company adds four digits to its parent's nine.

Search by UIC, not by name. The register stores company names in Cyrillic, and a search in Latin letters does not match the foreign-language spelling some records carry. If you only have a name, transliterate it into Cyrillic and expect several near-matches.

Check three things against what the supplier told you:

- the legal name and legal form (for example ООД or ЕООД for a limited company, АД or ЕАД for a joint-stock company; the "Е" means a single owner);
- the registered seat and address;
- any status words in the name, such as "в ликвидация" (in liquidation) or "в несъстоятелност" (in insolvency). The portal also has free bankruptcy and liquidation references.

## Minutes 5–12: who can sign, and who owns

Open the "State of play" view for the UIC. It shows the record as it stands today, or as it stood on any date you choose, which is useful when checking a contract signed months ago.

Look for:

- **Managers or representatives.** Confirm that the person signing your contract is listed and, for a board, that the mandate has not expired.
- **Partners or sole owner.** For limited companies these appear on the record.
- **Capital.** Since 1 January 2026 registered capital has been converted to euro automatically, so older documents in the file may still show leva.

Then reverse the search. The free reference by natural person or legal entity shows every other company in which the same person appears as owner or manager. A director who sits on a dozen recently formed companies, or a sole owner who also controls the supplier's main customer, is worth a question.

Beneficial owners are recorded in the register but are no longer open to everyone; see our separate note, [Who really owns a Bulgarian supplier?](insight-who-owns-a-bulgarian-supplier.html).

## Minutes 12–20: read the change history

Every field on the record carries the entry number that put it there, and that number begins with the date and time of the change. Scan for clustering:

- a new manager, new owner or new address entered shortly before your first order;
- several changes in a short period;
- a capital change with no obvious business reason.

The "Case" tab lists every application filed for the company and whether it was entered, refused or returned with instructions. A run of refusals can signal disputes or sloppy administration.

One caution: the Registry Agency has reported processing delays in 2026, including a large backlog of annual-statement filings, so a recent change may not yet appear. Ask the supplier directly about anything filed in the last few weeks.

## Minutes 20–27: open the accounts

Companies must publish annual financial statements in the register by 30 September of the following year. They sit under "Announced acts" and download free. Revenue and headcount are not fields on the record; they exist only inside these documents, usually scanned PDFs in Bulgarian.

What to look for:

- **Is the latest year there?** A missing year, or a declaration of no activity, is a signal in itself.
- **Does the scale fit the order?** A company reporting minimal revenue, staff or fixed assets that offers to deliver a large production run is likely a trader, not a producer.
- **Equity and liabilities.** Negative equity or a sharp jump in short-term debt deserves a question.

Small companies that are not audited only have to publish a balance sheet and notes, so a missing income statement is not by itself suspicious.

## Minutes 27–30: three outside checks

- **VAT.** The register does not show VAT status. Check the number in the EU's VIES service; a Bulgarian VAT number is usually "BG" plus the UIC.
- **Sanctions and adverse media.** Screen the company, its owners and managers against EU, UK and US sanctions lists (OpenSanctions aggregates them free), and run a Bulgarian-language news search on the names.
- **Claims on the website.** Note any certifications the supplier advertises (ISO 9001, AQAP or sector approvals) and verify them with the issuing body, not the PDF on the website.

## What the 30-minute check will not tell you

- **Ultimate beneficial owners**, unless you are an obliged entity or can show a legitimate interest.
- **The scanned documents behind each entry**, which need an electronic signature or a certificate issued by the Agency.
- **Pledges, property and court cases.** These sit in other registers and court systems; a full review should check them.
- **Whether the company actually makes what it sells.** Only a site visit, customer references or production evidence answers that.

## What this means for buyers

- Do you have the supplier's UIC in writing, and does the register record match the name, seat and signatory on your contract?
- Is the supplier a producer or a trader, judged by its own filed accounts rather than its brochure?
- Have ownership or management changed in the six months before your first order?
- Are the latest accounts filed, and does the company's size fit the order you are placing?
- Have you screened the owners and managers, not just the company name?
