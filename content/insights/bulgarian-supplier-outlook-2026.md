# Bulgarian Supplier Outlook 2026

Summary: Bulgaria enters 2027 as a lower-risk country but not yet a lower-risk supplier base. The euro, Schengen and Bulgaria's removal from the FATF list of jurisdictions under increased monitoring have reduced macro and banking frictions. The UK has extended the Lukoil Bulgaria general licence to 26 February 2027, but sanctions enforcement, ownership transparency and governance of state-linked companies are still catching up, so counterparty checks remain essential.
Published: October 2026
Date: 2026-10-23
Category: outlook

Our key judgements for the year to end-2027:

1. **Country risk is falling; counterparty risk is not.** Euro adoption on 1 January 2026 and FATF's removal of Bulgaria from increased monitoring on 30 October 2026 should ease payments and onboarding. Neither changes the owners, customers or governance of an individual supplier.
2. **Enforcement is tightening from a low base.** Corporate liability for customs, fraud and corruption offences has applied since 31 October 2025. A bill making sanctions breaches and circumvention a crime passed first reading on 24 September 2026. Suppliers with weak compliance face more legal risk than at any time since EU accession.
3. **Defence is the largest opportunity and the most political.** Exports and EU money are rising, but the Rheinmetall–VMZ reversal shows that state-linked commitments can be reopened when governments change.
4. **Russia exposure has narrowed to a few critical points.** The Burgas refinery remains dependent on sanctions licences. The UK licence for the four Lukoil Bulgarian entities now runs to 26 February 2027, while the US General Licence 130B runs to 24 April 2027. Bulgaria has pledged to end Russian gas transit, and EU sanctions end Russian LNG contracts by 1 January 2027.
5. **The eastern corridors are both the opportunity and the leak.** Trade with Turkey reached a record €9 billion in 2025, and the Middle Corridor is growing. The same routes carry most diverted goods; in April 2026 the EU used its anti-circumvention tool for the first time, against Kyrgyzstan.
6. **2027 is a compliance-deadline year.** EUDR (from 30 December 2026), the first CBAM certificate surrender, new EU anti-money-laundering access rules and recurring sanctions-licence renewals all land within twelve months.

## Operating environment

Stable politics and euro membership improve the backdrop; fiscal slippage and slow institutional reform limit how far it improves.

| Factor | Position in October 2026 | Direction for 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Politics | Single-party majority government since May 2026, after eight elections in five years; anti-corruption and judicial reform are stated priorities | More stable; reform delivery and foreign-policy direction uncertain |
| Growth | 3.1% in 2025; forecasts of about 2.5% for 2026, slowed by the Gulf energy shock | Moderate, not a breakout |
| Public finances | Deficit about 3–3.5% of GDP in 2025, forecast above 4% in 2026–27; debt about 30% of GDP | Pressure to consolidate; low debt gives room |
| Currency | Euro since 1 January 2026; changeover effect on inflation small | Settled |
| Anti-money laundering | Removed from FATF increased monitoring on 30 October 2026 after completing the action plan | Banking frictions may ease; MONEYVAL follow-up continues |
| Sanctions law | Corporate liability in force since October 2025; sanctions-crime bill at first reading | Tighter, from a low base |
| Ownership transparency | Beneficial-owner access restricted by law since July 2025; public view still partly open in practice | Narrower public access as EU rules bed in |
| State companies | 266 state enterprises; boards replaced at the energy holding and others after the change of government | Management turnover tied to politics |
| Corruption perception | 40/100, 84th of 182 (2025), worst since 2012 | Slow improvement at best |

The practical effect for buyers: payments, banking and currency are simpler than a year ago. Due diligence on owners, customers and state-linked counterparties is not.

## Scenarios for 2027

Our base case is gradual improvement with uneven enforcement. The probabilities are Pistiros judgements, not forecasts.

|  | Tighter and cleaner | Base: gradual improvement | Drift |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Probability | 20–25% | 55–60% | 20–25% |
| Rule of law | New judicial council and prosecutor general seen as independent | Appointments made, credibility contested | Reform stalls or is politicised |
| Sanctions | Sanctions-crime law adopted and used in visible cases | Law adopted, little enforcement yet | Law delayed; EU infringement escalates |
| Russia exposure | Burgas refinery sold or nationalised cleanly; gas transit ends | Licences keep rolling; transit ends on schedule | Licence lapse disrupts fuel supply |
| Defence | Restructured Rheinmetall project signed; SAFE money flowing | Talks continue; smaller contracts proceed | Project abandoned; investor confidence hit |
| Implication for buyers | Lighter checks justified for well-run suppliers | Current level of checks remains right | Heavier checks, more security in contracts |

### Indicators to watch

- **FATF follow-up**: whether Bulgaria sustains the reforms recognised in the 30 October 2026 removal decision.
- **Sanctions-crime bill**: second reading and first prosecutions.
- **Judicial appointments**: who is chosen for the Supreme Judicial Council and prosecutor general, and with what cross-party support.
- **Lukoil licences**: UK Lukoil Bulgaria licence (26 February 2027), UK Lukoil International licence (check separately), US General License 130B (24 April 2027).
- **Rheinmetall–VMZ**: a signed, funded agreement or a formal end to talks.
- **Kozloduy units 7 and 8**: signature of the main construction contract.
- **EU anti-circumvention measures**: any further country listings after Kyrgyzstan.
- **Ownership changes** at state companies and large private suppliers after each political shift.

## Buyer checklist for 2027

A sound Bulgarian supplier in 2027 can show the following without being chased. Use it as a minimum before placing significant orders.

**Identity and ownership**

- [ ] Registry record (UIC, name, seat, signatories) matches the contract
- [ ] Ownership chart down to named individuals, with registry extracts
- [ ] No owner, manager or key counterparty on EU, UK or US sanctions lists, including US 50% and UK control tests
- [ ] No recent ownership or management change left unexplained

**Substance and finances**

- [ ] Latest annual accounts filed, and company size fits the order
- [ ] Producer, not just a trader, where that is what you are paying for
- [ ] Certifications confirmed with the issuing body

**Export controls and sanctions**

- [ ] Products classified against EU dual-use and Russia ban lists
- [ ] Registered with the Ministry of Economy as a dual-use exporter where required
- [ ] No-re-export-to-Russia clause in non-EU sales of listed goods
- [ ] Named person responsible for compliance, with a written procedure
- [ ] Customers and end users known, especially in Turkey, the Caucasus and Central Asia

**Sector-specific**

- [ ] Timber: plot-level geolocation ready for EUDR
- [ ] Steel, aluminium, fertilisers: verified emissions data for CBAM
- [ ] Agri-food and essential oils: independent authenticity testing
- [ ] Electronics and drones: bill of materials with country of origin
- [ ] Fuel: current Lukoil licence status checked if relevant

**Contract**

- [ ] Dispute forum chosen (arbitration seat or named courts)
- [ ] Security proportionate to exposure
- [ ] Audit, termination and sanctions-breach clauses
