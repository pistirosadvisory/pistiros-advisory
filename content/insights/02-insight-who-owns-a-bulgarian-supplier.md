# Who really owns a Bulgarian supplier?

Summary: Knowing who ultimately owns and controls a supplier is the core of sanctions and diversion checks. Since a 2022 EU court ruling, beneficial-ownership registers across Europe have become harder to read, and Bulgaria is changing its rules too. Here is what a foreign buyer can still see, and how to fill the gaps.
Published: October 2026
Date: 2026-10-25
Category: guides

## The 2022 ruling and what followed

EU anti-money-laundering law had required member states to make beneficial-ownership registers open to the general public. On 22 November 2022, in joined cases C-37/20 and C-601/20, the Court of Justice of the EU struck down that requirement, holding that unrestricted public access was a serious interference with privacy and data protection that was neither strictly necessary nor proportionate ([eucrim](https://eucrim.eu/news/cjeu-no-unrestricted-access-to-data-of-beneficial-owners/)). Several countries closed or restricted their registers within days ([Kyckr](https://kyckr.com/guides-and-reports/eu-ubo-register-access-for-obliged-entities)).

The EU's 2024 anti-money-laundering package responded by replacing open access with access for those who can show a "legitimate interest". The relevant directive, (EU) 2024/1640, set a July 2026 deadline for member states to make that access work and a final deadline of 10 July 2027 for all provisions to be in force ([Kyckr](https://kyckr.com/guides-and-reports/eu-ubo-register-access-for-obliged-entities)). How "legitimate interest" is tested varies by country and is still settling.

## Where Bulgaria stands

Bulgaria does not run a separate beneficial-ownership database. Under its Measures Against Money Laundering Act, ownership data sits inside each company's record in the Commercial Register ([Kyckr](https://kyckr.com/guides-and-reports/bulgaria-company-registry)).

The legal position and the practical position currently differ:

- **The law.** Bulgaria amended the act in July 2025 to limit access to competent authorities, obliged entities carrying out due diligence, and persons with a legitimate interest ([Kyckr](https://kyckr.com/guides-and-reports/eu-ubo-register-access-for-obliged-entities); [CompanyData](https://companydata.com/company-register/bulgaria/)).
- **The practice.** When Kyckr checked the register on 23 July 2026, beneficial owners' names, ownership percentages and countries of residence were still visible without proving an interest. Residential addresses, personal identification numbers and certified documents required a qualified electronic signature, a Bulgarian personal identification code, or a certificate obtained in person from the Registry Agency.
- **Enforcement.** Kyckr reports that Bulgaria was among 18 member states sent a letter of formal notice by the European Commission in September 2026 for not fully notifying transposition. We have not seen the Commission's notice directly.

Treat what is visible today as subject to change. A check that works this month may not work next year.

## What remains public

Even without beneficial-ownership access, the free Commercial Register shows a good deal:

- **Limited companies (ООД, ЕООД).** Partners and their shares are on the record. For a single-owner ЕООД the owner is usually visible. If the owner is an individual, you may already have your answer.
- **Joint-stock companies (АД, ЕАД).** Shareholders are generally not listed on the public record. Listed companies disclose major holders through the stock exchange and the Financial Supervision Commission; unlisted ones keep their own share books. This is where ownership becomes hard to see.
- **Directors and representatives** for every form, plus every other company where the same person appears.

The most common gap is a chain: a Bulgarian company owned by another Bulgarian АД, owned in turn by a company in Cyprus, the UAE or elsewhere. Each link may be visible in its own register, or not at all.

## How to fill the gaps

- **Ask the supplier.** A legitimate supplier should provide an ownership chart down to individuals, with supporting registry extracts. Reluctance is information.
- **Use your status.** If you are a bank, payment firm or other obliged entity, you may have formal access for due diligence. A legitimate-interest route may also open for others as the new rules bed in.
- **Follow the chain in each country.** Search the parent companies in their own registers. EU registers are linked through the Business Registers Interconnection System, which helps with basic company data but not ownership.
- **Cross-check people, not just companies.** Search each owner and director across Bulgarian companies and in leaked-data collections such as the ICIJ Offshore Leaks database.
- **Watch for control without ownership.** Nominee shareholders, powers of attorney, and a manager who appears in many unrelated companies can all mean the person in charge is not the person on the record.

## Red flags in ownership

- The ownership chain ends in a jurisdiction with no public register, and the supplier will not provide documents.
- Ownership or management changed shortly before your first order, or after a new sanctions package.
- The same individual appears as manager of many recently formed companies at the same address.
- An АД structure where a small supplier has no business reason for one.
- Owners or directors with links to companies in Russia, Belarus or known transit hubs.

## What this means for buyers

- Can the supplier give you a documented ownership chart that ends in named individuals?
- Does any link in the chain sit in a register you cannot see, and what evidence replaces it?
- Have you screened every owner and director, not only the company name?
- Is your organisation an obliged entity, and if so, are you using the access the law gives you?
- When will you re-check? Ownership is a snapshot, not a permanent fact.
