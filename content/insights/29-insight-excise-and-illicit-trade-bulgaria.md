# Excise and illicit trade: tobacco, fuel and brand protection

Summary: Excise goods (tobacco, fuel and alcohol) carry high taxes, which makes them a constant target for smuggling and fraud. Bulgaria's own market is relatively clean on cigarettes, but it is a notable source of cheap cigarettes that turn up illegally elsewhere in Europe, and fuel fraud has a long history. For brand owners, distributors and logistics firms, the risk is being used as a link in someone else's scheme.

## Tobacco: a clean home market, an outbound problem

KPMG's annual study of illicit cigarette consumption in Europe, commissioned by Philip Morris, estimated for 2024 ([KPMG](https://monitoring.economx.hu/documents/19/illicit-cigarette-consumption-in-europe-2024-results-1-1.pdf)):

- **EU-wide:** 38.9 billion counterfeit and contraband cigarettes consumed, 9.2% of all consumption and the highest level in a decade, costing about €14.9 billion in lost tax.
- **Bulgaria:** only about 1% of domestic consumption was counterfeit or contraband, among the lowest shares in Europe.
- **Bulgaria as a source:** flows of "illicit whites" (cheap brands made legally in one country and smuggled into others where they have no legal distribution) from Bulgaria were the main driver of growth in known-origin illicit whites across the markets studied.

The study is industry-funded and its method relies on collecting discarded packs, so treat the figures as indicative. The pattern is still clear: cigarettes produced or sold in Bulgaria are reaching other markets through illegal channels.

## Fuel

Fuel fraud in Bulgaria has taken several forms over the years: undeclared imports, fake delivery documents, misdeclaring fuel as lower-taxed oils or solvents, illegal mini-refineries and VAT fraud. Since 2014 a fuel-control system has tracked fuel from production or import through to sales at filling stations, with each delivery carrying a unique number that the Customs Agency and National Revenue Agency check in real time. The first cases it caught involved deliveries carrying numbers that did not exist ([BTA](https://www.bta.bg/en/news/189149-Software-Helps-Customs-Agency-National-Revenue-Agency-Detect-Fuel-Fraud)).

The fuel market is also shaped by the sanctions licences for the Lukoil-owned Burgas refinery, which keep being extended for short periods. Disruption there would change supply routes and create openings for irregular imports.

## Customs and governance

Excise enforcement depends on customs and tax agencies whose leadership changes with governments. For buyers, the point is that enforcement quality varies over time, so a counterparty's clean record with the authorities is less informative than its own controls.

## How legitimate businesses get drawn in

- **Distributors** whose product is bought in volume by a customer and resold into markets the brand does not supply.
- **Logistics firms** hired to move excise goods under suspended-duty arrangements, with paperwork that does not match the load.
- **Warehouse operators** holding excise goods in tax-suspended warehouses for parties they have not checked.
- **Brand owners** whose genuine packaging or production equipment ends up with counterfeiters.
- **Fuel buyers** offered prices well below market, which usually means someone has not paid tax.

## What brand owners and buyers can do

- **Know your distributors' customers**, especially for excise goods, and watch for volumes that exceed local demand.
- **Use track-and-trace data.** EU tobacco traceability rules give each pack a unique identifier; check that your supply chain records match.
- **Check excise authorisations** of warehouses and traders you use.
- **Monitor online marketplaces and neighbouring markets** for your product appearing where you do not sell.
- **Write audit and termination rights** into distribution agreements.
- **Treat below-market fuel or tobacco offers as red flags**, not bargains.

## What this means for buyers

- If you sell excise goods through Bulgaria, do you know where your distributor's customers resell?
- Do the volumes your Bulgarian partners buy match legitimate demand in the markets they serve?
- Are the warehouses and hauliers moving your goods authorised and screened?
- Would you know if your product appeared illegally in another country?
