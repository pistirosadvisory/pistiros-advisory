# Kapitan Andreevo and the EU–Turkey land border

Summary: Kapitan Andreevo, on the Bulgarian side, and Kapıkule, on the Turkish side, form one of the busiest land freight crossings in Europe. For buyers moving goods between Turkey and the EU by road, delay here is a cost to plan for, not an exception.
Published: October 2026
Date: 2026-10-25
Category: regional

## The scale

Turkish government figures cited by GetTransport put 2025 traffic at Kapıkule at about 4 million vehicles, including over 1 million heavy goods vehicles. The single-day record cited was 3,442 commercial vehicles on 7 December, of which 1,753 were heading into Turkey ([GetTransport](https://blog.gettransport.com/trends-in-logistic/busiest-land-border-crossings-europe-2026/)).

On a typical day Bulgarian customs is reported to handle around 3,000 trucks, with truck waiting times cited at 24 to 72 hours, up from a previous range of 12 to 48 hours. Neither figure carries a clear date in the source, so treat them as indicative ([GetTransport](https://blog.gettransport.com/trends-in-logistic/busiest-land-border-crossings-europe-2026/)).

Three Bulgaria–Turkey crossings take freight: Kapitan Andreevo–Kapıkule, Lesovo–Hamzabeyli, and Malko Tarnovo–Dereköy, which is smaller. Lesovo is the usual relief route but has its own limits. On 23 June 2026 a 23 km truck queue formed on the Turkish side at Hamzabeyli; drivers complained there was nowhere to park while waiting, so the queue sat on the road ([BTA](https://www.bta.bg/en/news/balkans/1152995-23-km-truck-queue-forms-at-bulgarian-turkish-border-crossing)).

## Why goods stop at a customs-union border

Turkey and the EU have had a customs union for most industrial goods since 1996. That removes duties on qualifying goods but not the border:

- **It is the EU's external border.** Bulgaria applies EU customs, safety and security rules on entry, plus import VAT.
- **Free circulation must be proven.** Industrial goods usually need an A.TR movement certificate to enter duty-free. Wrong or missing documents mean duty, delay or both.
- **Agricultural goods are largely outside the union.** Fresh produce and food face duties, quotas and plant and food-safety checks.
- **Controlled goods need licences.** Dual-use items, defence goods and sanctioned goods are checked against permits and lists.
- **Enforcement attention is high.** In December 2023 the Bulgarian government announced 100% inspection of lorries on this route after finding dual-use goods moving through it towards Armenia, Azerbaijan and onward to Russia ([BNT](https://bntnews.bg/news/huge-upsurge-in-the-import-export-of-dual-use-goods-to-azerbaijan-and-russia-routed-through-bulgaria-updated-1304377news.html)). We have not seen how long that level of inspection was maintained.

Schengen membership since January 2025 removed routine checks at Bulgaria's borders with Greece and Romania. It did not change the Turkish border, which remains an external one.

## What drives delay

- **Volume** against a plaza that was not designed for it. Textiles, auto parts, white goods, produce and steel all share the same lanes.
- **Documentation errors** that send trucks to a secondary check.
- **Peaks** before weekends and holidays, and at seasonal highs for produce.
- **Industrial action and policy changes**, on either side, which can stop traffic with little notice.

Capacity projects are under way, including dedicated heavy-goods crossings and modernisation of the existing plaza, but they will not help near-term bookings ([GetTransport](https://blog.gettransport.com/trends-in-logistic/busiest-land-border-crossings-europe-2026/)).

## How buyers and sellers plan around it

- **Build the border into lead times.** Price two to three days of potential waiting into delivery schedules and driver costs, then tighten as you gather your own data.
- **Get documents right before dispatch.** A.TR certificates, commercial invoices, packing lists, HS codes and any licences should match exactly. Most avoidable delay is paperwork.
- **Use authorised status where you can.** Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) status and simplified procedures can reduce checks; ask your forwarder what applies to your goods.
- **Watch live border status.** Wait times change by the hour. Check on the day of dispatch.
- **Consider alternatives on tight timelines.** Ro-Ro sea services from Turkish ports to Italy, Greece or Trieste bypass the Balkan land route; whether they are cheaper depends on the load.

## The integrity side

A busy border cuts both ways. Most traffic is ordinary trade. The same volume makes it attractive for moving controlled goods under ordinary-looking paperwork, which is why enforcement attention is high. For a buyer, that means:

- your goods may be inspected more closely than you expect, so documentation needs to be complete;
- a counterparty that offers to "handle the border" through personal contacts is a red flag, not a service.

## What this means for buyers

- Does your delivery schedule include realistic border time, and who bears the cost when it runs over?
- Who prepares your A.TR certificates and customs documents, and how often are they rejected?
- Are any of your goods controlled, and does the licence cover this route and destination?
- Does your forwarder use Kapitan Andreevo, Lesovo or sea routes, and how does it decide?
- If goods pass through Bulgaria in transit, who controls them at each stage?
