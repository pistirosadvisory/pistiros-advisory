# Ukraine reconstruction from Bulgaria

Summary: Rebuilding Ukraine will be one of the largest construction and engineering programmes in Europe for a decade. Bulgaria sits close by, across the Black Sea and the Danube, with energy, transport and construction firms keen to take part. Politics, security and the integrity of procurement will decide who benefits.
Published: October 2026
Date: 2026-10-25
Category: regional

## The scale

The fifth joint assessment by the World Bank, UN, EU and Ukrainian government, released in February 2026 with data to the end of 2025, put Ukraine's recovery and reconstruction needs at almost $588 billion over the next ten years, about three times Ukraine's estimated 2025 GDP. Direct damage exceeded $195 billion. The largest needs are ([UNDP](https://www.undp.org/ukraine/press-releases/updated-ukraine-recovery-and-reconstruction-needs-assessment-released)):

| Sector | Estimated needs |
| --- | --- |
| Transport | over $96 billion |
| Energy | nearly $91 billion |
| Housing | almost $90 billion |
| Commerce and industry | over $63 billion |

Money comes from the EU's Ukraine Facility, multilateral development banks, bilateral donors, private investment and, potentially, proceeds linked to frozen Russian assets.

## Bulgaria's pitch

At the 2025 Ukraine Recovery Conference in Rome, Bulgaria's then prime minister set out areas where Bulgarian companies could take part ([Government of Bulgaria](https://gov.bg/en/Press-center/News/Prime-Minister-Jeliazkov-Bulgaria-can-participate-in-numerous-projects-for-the-recovery-of-Ukraine)):

- **Energy**: the Vertical Gas Corridor carrying gas from Greece through Bulgaria and Romania to Ukraine, and Black Sea electricity and data links.
- **Transport**: a proposed rail link from the Greek port of Alexandroupolis to Odesa through Bulgaria and Romania, a "Black Sea motorway" from Turkey to Ukraine, intermodal use of Bulgarian ports, and a third Danube bridge with Romania.
- **Dual-use infrastructure** linked to NATO spending commitments.

A year earlier, Bulgaria said more than 80 of its companies were ready to help rebuild Ukraine's energy sector, and others had expressed interest in road building ([BNT](https://bnt.bg/news/80-bulgarian-companies-ready-to-help-rebuild-ukraine-39s-energy-sector-329886news.html)).

## The political factor

Bulgaria's government changed in May 2026. The new defence minister said in June that Bulgaria would send no more weapons to Ukraine, as covered in our [defence-industry note](insight-bulgaria-defence-market-outlook.html), and the government has called for a negotiated end to the war. Civilian reconstruction is a different matter from military aid, and the government has continued to promote the gas corridor, but Bulgarian firms' access to Ukrainian projects may depend partly on how relations between the two governments develop.

## Practical realities

- **Procurement rules.** Most donor-funded contracts follow World Bank, EBRD, EIB or EU procurement rules, with strict integrity, beneficial-ownership and sanctions checks. Firms that cannot pass them will not win.
- **Security.** Work in Ukraine carries physical risk, insurance costs and the possibility of renewed strikes on infrastructure.
- **Local partners.** Ukrainian partners need checking like any other: ownership, links to sanctioned persons, and past integrity issues.
- **Corruption risk.** Ukraine has its own anti-corruption bodies and donors scrutinise spending closely, but large inflows of money attract misuse on both sides of any border.
- **Logistics.** Danube and Black Sea routes face war-risk insurance, mines and port congestion.

## Where Bulgarian firms fit

- **Energy equipment and engineering**, including grid, substations and gas infrastructure.
- **Construction materials and road building**, especially in western and southern Ukraine.
- **Logistics** through Ruse, Varna and Burgas.
- **Subcontracting** to larger Western contractors, which often need regional partners.

## What this means for buyers and partners

- If you plan to bid on Ukrainian reconstruction with a Bulgarian partner, can that partner pass development-bank integrity checks?
- Does your Ukrainian counterparty have any links to sanctioned persons or past investigations?
- How will you handle security, insurance and force majeure?
- Are you relying on a political relationship between governments that could change?
