# Central Asia as markets, not just transit

Summary: Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and their neighbours are often discussed only as stops on the Middle Corridor. They are also fast-moving markets and suppliers of raw materials the EU wants. Since April 2026 one of them, Kyrgyzstan, has become the first country the EU has targeted with trade restrictions for helping Russia evade sanctions. Opportunity and risk sit side by side.
Published: October 2026
Date: 2026-10-25
Category: regional

## The EU's push

At the first EU–Central Asia summit in Samarkand in April 2025, the two sides upgraded relations to a strategic partnership and the EU announced a €12 billion investment package ([OSW](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/analyses/2025-04-09/eu-central-asia-summit-samarkand-economy-comes-first)):

- **€6.4 billion** for environmental projects, including hydropower;
- **€3 billion** for the Middle Corridor;
- **€2.5 billion** for critical raw materials exploration and extraction;
- **€100 million** for satellite connectivity.

Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan have become more important EU partners, largely through oil and uranium exports from Kazakhstan. China remains the region's main trading partner and Russia its main security reference point; the region's governments committed to preventing sanctions circumvention without naming Russia ([OSW](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/analyses/2025-04-09/eu-central-asia-summit-samarkand-economy-comes-first)).

## Where the opportunities are

- **Raw materials.** Kazakhstan's oil, uranium and metals, and the EU's interest in critical raw materials, create supply and processing opportunities. Bulgaria's copper smelter at Pirdop already relies on imported concentrate.
- **Infrastructure and energy.** EU-funded hydropower, grids and transport projects need contractors, equipment and engineering services.
- **Consumer and industrial goods.** Uzbekistan's market of over 35 million people has opened considerably since 2017.
- **Logistics.** Bulgarian ports and forwarders can position as the EU end of Middle Corridor cargo, competing with Romania and Turkey.

## Where the risk is concentrated: Kyrgyzstan

Trade data have pointed to Kyrgyzstan as a route for goods reaching Russia. Georgian statistics showed $964 million of cars re-exported to Kyrgyzstan in the first ten months of 2024, while Kyrgyz statistics recorded only about $50 million of cars arriving from Georgia ([Eurasianet](https://eurasianet.org/georgia-and-kyrgyzstan-suspected-of-being-auto-suppliers-for-russia-trade-data-indicates)).

The EU responded in its 20th sanctions package, adopted on 23 April 2026. For the first time it used its anti-circumvention tool against a third country, banning EU sales and exports to Kyrgyzstan of metal-working machining centres (CN 8457 10) and certain data-transmission and routing equipment (CN 8517 62). The same package imposed transaction bans on several financial institutions outside Russia, with locations reported to include Kyrgyzstan, designated a Kyrgyz crypto exchange trading a state-backed stablecoin, and banned transactions involving the rouble-linked A7A5 stablecoin and other Russian digital-currency instruments ([Baker McKenzie](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/eu-implements-20th-sanctions-package-against-russia/)).

Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan are not under similar restrictions, but entities based in Central Asia appear among third-country companies designated for supplying Russia's military industry ([Baker McKenzie](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/eu-implements-20th-sanctions-package-against-russia/)).

## Practical rules for selling into the region

- **Check country-specific bans.** Some goods can now no longer be sold to Kyrgyzstan at all. Check the current annexes before quoting.
- **Classify every product** against the EU's Russia ban lists and the G7 high-priority list.
- **Verify the end user** and that order sizes fit the local market.
- **Screen banks and payment providers.** Third-country banks and crypto platforms are now being designated; a payment routed through one can be a breach.
- **Watch digital-currency payments.** Rouble-linked stablecoins are banned instruments under EU sanctions.
- **Include the no-re-export-to-Russia clause** for listed goods.

## What this means for buyers and sellers

- Are any of your products now banned for sale to Kyrgyzstan, and does your distributor in Kazakhstan or Uzbekistan sell on there?
- Do you know which banks and payment channels your Central Asian customers use?
- Could an EU-funded project in the region be a lower-risk entry point than private distribution?
- If you source raw materials from the region, can you trace them to the mine?
