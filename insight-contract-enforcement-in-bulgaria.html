Contract enforcement in Bulgaria: courts, arbitration and realistic timelines

A contract with a Bulgarian supplier or customer is only as good as your ability to enforce it. Bulgarian courts work, but slowly, and collecting on a judgment can take longer than winning it. Foreign companies should decide how disputes will be resolved before they sign.

October 2026 · This article is in English.

How long things take

Indicative timelines from practitioner sources:

StepTypical durationSource
Order-for-payment procedure (undisputed claims)About one week to two monthsAllianz Trade
Interim measures to freeze assets1–7 daysAllianz Trade
First-instance court caseAbout 12 months if straightforward; 2–3 years with experts and witnessesGugushev & Partners
AppealAbout 1 yearAllianz Trade
Cassation6 months to 1 yearAllianz Trade
Enforcement against assetsCan exceed five years, depending on the debtorAllianz Trade
InsolvencyOften three to five years or more, with little for unsecured creditorsAllianz Trade

A contested claim that goes through all three instances can take three to four years before enforcement starts.

Costs and interest

  • Court fees are about 4% of the claim at first instance and 2% on appeal and cassation; the order-for-payment procedure costs about 2%.
  • Statutory late-payment interest applies without a contract clause. From 1 January 2026 it is set at the ECB main refinancing rate plus 8 percentage points.
  • Commercial payment terms are capped at 60 days except in exceptional cases; where no term is agreed, payment is due 14 days after an invoice or reminder.
  • General claims are time-barred after five years; interest and damages after three (Allianz Trade).

The court system

Commercial disputes go to the ordinary courts: 113 regional courts, 28 district courts, 5 civil courts of appeal and the Supreme Court of Cassation. There is no separate commercial court. Smaller claims start in regional courts, larger ones in district courts (Gugushev & Partners).

Proceedings are in Bulgarian, foreign documents need certified translation, and evidence from expert witnesses is common and slow. Digital filing systems exist but are not yet used to their full potential.

Judicial independence has been a long-running concern, and reform of the Supreme Judicial Council is a stated priority of the government elected in April 2026. For high-value disputes with politically connected counterparties, that is a reason to consider arbitration.

Arbitration

Arbitration is usually the better route for foreign companies:

  • Neutral seat and language. You can choose a seat outside Bulgaria and English as the language.
  • Enforceability. Bulgaria is a party to the New York Convention, so foreign awards can be enforced through Bulgarian courts subject to limited grounds for refusal.
  • Local option. The Arbitration Court at the Bulgarian Chamber of Commerce and Industry is the main domestic institution. International institutions such as the ICC, LCIA or Vienna International Arbitral Centre are common choices for larger contracts.

Arbitration costs more up front and still needs Bulgarian courts for enforcement. For small, undisputed debts, the order-for-payment procedure is faster and cheaper.

Enforcing judgments and awards

  • EU judgments are generally recognised and enforced in Bulgaria without special proceedings (Gugushev & Partners).
  • Non-EU judgments, including UK judgments since Brexit, are checked against public policy, jurisdiction and the defendant's right to participate before enforcement.
  • Enforcement is carried out by state or private bailiffs, who can seize bank accounts, movable property and real estate. Appeal court decisions are enforceable even if a further appeal is pending.

Protect yourself before signing

  • Choose the forum. Arbitration with an agreed seat, or the courts of a named country, written into the contract.
  • Take security. Advance payments, letters of credit, bank guarantees or pledges registered in Bulgaria.
  • Check the counterparty's assets. Filed accounts show whether a company has anything to enforce against.
  • Use notarised acknowledgements where possible; some qualify for faster enforcement.
  • Watch limitation periods and send formal reminders, which start statutory interest.
  • Check the signatory's authority in the Commercial Register. A contract signed by someone without power to bind the company is weak from the start.

What this means for buyers

  • Does your contract name a forum, and is it one you could realistically use?
  • Is the supplier's balance sheet large enough to pay a judgment?
  • Have you taken security proportionate to your exposure?
  • If you are a UK company, have you allowed for the extra step of recognising a UK judgment in Bulgaria?
