Metals and mining: Bulgaria's copper economy and the EU raw-materials push

Copper is Bulgaria's largest export, ahead of arms and grain. One smelter at Pirdop dominates the trade, mines supply part of the ore, and the rest is imported. With the EU treating copper as a strategic raw material, the sector is attracting public money and closer scrutiny of where ore comes from.

October 2026 · This article is in English.

The scale

Analysis of 2024 trade data by Capital shows (Capital):

  • Copper and copper products: a record €4.2 billion, nearly 10% of Bulgaria's €43 billion of goods exports, from more than 433,000 tonnes, mostly refined and unrefined copper. Bulgaria is among the EU's top three copper exporters.
  • Metal ores and concentrates (excluding gold and silver): over €736 million, the largest in the EU.
  • Destinations: more than two-thirds of copper goes to Germany, Italy and Turkey.

The Pirdop smelter

The Aurubis plant at Pirdop, owned by the German copper group, is Bulgaria's largest company by revenue, with sales of about €4.1 billion in 2022. It processes all copper concentrate produced in Bulgaria, which covers only about 30% of its capacity; the remaining 70% is imported (Capital).

Aurubis has a €400 million investment programme at the site to end-2027, including a €120 million expansion of its refinery to raise cathode capacity by about half, to 340,000 tonnes a year, with new capacity starting in the second half of 2026 (Capital). The European Investment Bank has assessed financing for the Pirdop expansion as a critical-raw-materials project, on condition that Aurubis publishes responsible-sourcing audit reports for the site (EIB).

Domestic mining

Bulgarian ore comes from open-pit copper mines in the Srednogorie belt, including Elatsite and Assarel, and from Dundee Precious Metals' underground Chelopech mine, which produces copper-gold concentrate. Dundee also operates the Ada Tepe gold mine in the Rhodopes. Mining in Bulgaria is concentrated in a few large operators, and new projects face long permitting and local opposition.

Why the EU cares

Copper is a strategic raw material under the EU's Critical Raw Materials Act, which sets targets for EU extraction, processing and recycling and lets designated projects access faster permits and financing. A refinery that can process more of Europe's copper inside the EU fits that policy directly, which is why Pirdop is attracting public lenders.

Copper is not in scope of the EU carbon border charge (CBAM), but aluminium and steel are. Metal processors that also buy those materials should read our CBAM note.

Where the risks sit

  • Imported concentrate. With 70% of Pirdop's feed imported, the origin of ore matters. Concentrate traded globally can come from mines with environmental, labour or conflict concerns, and from sanctioned jurisdictions if checks fail. Aurubis's responsible-sourcing audits are a condition of EIB financing for that reason.
  • Concentration. Much of the country's metals exposure rests on one plant. Buyers of Bulgarian copper are, in effect, buying from Aurubis, which carries a lower counterparty risk but a single-site operational risk.
  • Downstream processors. Smaller Bulgarian firms making copper sheet, wire and profiles buy cathode or scrap and sell into automotive and electronics chains. Their scrap sourcing and customer lists deserve checks.
  • Diversion. Copper products and machine tools appear on the G7 high-priority list for Russia. A metal fabricator with sudden sales to Turkey or the Caucasus warrants questions.
  • Environment and community. Mining and smelting face local opposition, water and tailings issues, and EU scrutiny.

What this means for buyers

  • If you buy Bulgarian copper or copper products, do you know the origin of the concentrate or scrap behind them?
  • Does your supplier publish, or will it share, responsible-sourcing audits?
  • For smaller processors: who supplies their feed, and where do their products go?
  • If you are an investor, does the project qualify for, or depend on, EU critical-raw-materials support?
